Yves Ducharme

Former mayor of Hull, first mayor of the amalgamated City of Gatineau and past president of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities. A career in the service of cities, from the Outaouais to the national stage.

Career

From Hull to the Federation of Canadian Municipalities

  • 1958

    Born in Montreal

    He enters municipal politics in Hull, in the Outaouais.

  • 1986

    Hull city councillor

    Elected in the Mont-Bleu district, he sits on council until 1992.

  • 1992

    Mayor of Hull

    In office from December 6, 1992 to December 31, 2001, until the City of Hull ceases to exist in the municipal merger.

  • 2002

    First mayor of the new City of Gatineau

    Elected in November 2001, he leads the city created by merging Hull, Gatineau, Aylmer, Buckingham and Masson-Angers. He is mayor from January 1, 2002 to November 5, 2005.

  • 2003

    66th president of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities

    During his term, the FCM general assembly unanimously adopts his proposal for a tax credit to encourage travel within Canada.

  • 2006

    Order of the Aztec Eagle (Venera grade)

    An honour of the Government of Mexico, awarded on August 10, 2006 for his role in strengthening Canada–Mexico relations and supporting the creation of UNAM Gatineau.

  • 2026

    Corridor Intelligence and Finance Canada

    He files a proposal with Finance Canada for a tax credit for travelling in Canada and discusses it in an 18-minute interview.

Interview · Corridor Intelligence · 18 minutes · in French

A tax credit for travelling in Canada

In the first interview episode of Corridor Intelligence, Joseph Soares’s analysis program, Mr. Ducharme explains the reasoning behind his proposal and the conditions for it to work: the entire stay must take place in a province other than one’s own or the one next door, over a five-year program.

“If it’s too complicated to get your refund, people will give up. It has to be simple.”Yves Ducharme

The conversation also covers the federal decision by which Paul Martin freed municipalities from the GST, and the place of cities in Canadian political discourse.
